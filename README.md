# Mud & Crowns: a medieval life

A one-file, text-based medieval life simulator. Be born a serf or a princess,
work, wed, scheme, fight, and pass your lands to your children.

Open `index.html` in any browser to play. Progress is saved in your browser.

## Work and occupations

The **Work** panel lists more than a hundred posts in four tabs:

- **Common**: farm laborer, peasant, serf, shepherd, herder, stable hand,
  swineherd, cowherd, fisherman, hunter, woodcutter, charcoal burner, miner,
  quarry worker, ditch digger, road worker, construction laborer, porter, hauler,
  dock worker, washerwoman, laundress, scullery worker, kitchen helper,
  maidservant, house servant, messenger, beggar, street vendor, gravedigger,
  and assistants to tanners, smiths and millers.
- **Skilled**: blacksmith, weaponsmith, armorer, carpenter, mason, stonecutter,
  wheelwright, cooper, potter, weaver, tailor, shoemaker, tanner, baker,
  butcher, brewer, miller, candlemaker, ropemaker, fletcher, bowyer,
  leatherworker, jeweler, goldsmith, silversmith, barber, barber-surgeon,
  apothecary, merchant, shopkeeper, innkeeper, cook, scribe, clerk, accountant,
  sailor, shipwright, navigator, town watchman, guild member, master craftsman,
  schoolteacher.
- **Elite**: royal adviser, chancellor, treasurer, magistrate, judge, high
  officer of state, diplomat, court official, priest, monk, abbot/abbess,
  bishop, archbishop, cardinal, scholar, university professor, physician,
  steward, castellan, landowner, wealthy merchant, guild master, master
  architect. Lord, baron, count, duke, prince and king are ranks you win by
  birth, land, favour or the sword, and the tab explains how to get each one.
- **Military**: camp laborer, camp porter, archer, spearman, foot soldier,
  crossbowman, man-at-arms, sergeant, veteran soldier, knight's squire,
  captain, mercenary leader, knight, knight commander, baron at arms, marshal,
  general, lord high constable, and a duke at the head of an army.

Every year in a line of work adds to your **experience**. Experience, along with
your stats, letters, silver and birth, decides which posts you can get. Good
work gets you promoted along a career path (for example, smith's assistant,
blacksmith, master blacksmith, guild member, master craftsman, guild master).
Some posts raise your family's social class. A wealthy merchant or a
chancellor can outrank a poor knight, because in the Middle Ages wealth and
office counted for as much as birth.

## From mud to crown

Anyone can become king or queen. The menu's **Roads to the crown** section
tracks your progress on each road:

1. **Rebellion**: raise an army (poor folk can *Rouse the commons* for free once
   they have Renown 30), take your home county, then march on your king's royal
   seat.
2. **Royal marriage**: with Renown 55 and Looks 60, the monarch may court you.
   Outlive your royal spouse and the crown is yours.
3. **The sword in the anvil**: a rare event between 12 and 45.
4. **Inheritance**: be born royal, or carry on as your children.

A hint line above the buttons always says what to do next. You can turn it off
in the menu.

## Realism, power and fun

- **The rules of the age** (set at birth, or in the menu): under *Historical*
  rules the army, the priesthood, the universities and the offices of law are
  closed to women, and a few trades (midwife, wet nurse, silkwoman,
  lady-in-waiting) are women's alone. A woman may cut her hair and pass as a
  man to serve in arms or at sea, at the risk of being unmasked. Widows can
  run a business in their own name. *Relaxed* rules open everything to
  everyone.
- **Ages**: children can take small jobs from 7 (crow scarer, goose girl,
  page, chorister), most trades open at 12, and heavy work gets harder past 55.
- **Influence** (⚜️): your power to make things happen. It comes from your
  station, titles, office, wealth, Renown, army and friends. High posts need
  it, and so do the new *Power* deeds: call in favours, place your kin, arrange
  grand marriages, lean on tenants, and pull strings to get out of prison. Tap
  it to see what makes up yours.
- **Ambitions**: at 16 you choose a life goal. Fulfil it for a big reward.
- **Friends and rivals**: friends lend silver, cheer you up, put in a good
  word, and can become spouses. Rivals cheat you and spread rumours. Make
  peace, or ruin them.
- Many new events: royal progresses, May Day, a secret noble father, the Feast
  of Fools, dancing bears, secret admirers, shipwrecks, and more.

## War

- **Troops**: levies, archers, crossbowmen, men-at-arms, knights on horse, and
  siege engines, each with its own power, cost and upkeep. Casualties fall
  hardest on levies.
- **Terrain and walls**: horse charges win on plains and fail in marsh and
  forest. Archers love hills. Counties can have a palisade, stone walls or a
  great castle; siege engines (and gunpowder) break them down.
- **Tactics**: storm, siege, arrow volley, cavalry charge, night attack,
  mining, sending a captain, or demanding surrender, each showing its odds.
  Battles can turn on a traitor, fog, panic or a heroic stand.
- **After the battle**: sack the town, spare it, or ransom the lord (who may
  become your rival). Lose while leading and you may be captured.
- **Raids, spies, walls, diplomacy**: raid neighbours for loot, send spies to
  count a garrison, fortify your counties, offer truces, and demand tribute
  as a ruler. A war chronicle records it all.
- **The map** is drawn like an old chart, with terrain, borders, coastlines,
  a compass and kingdom names. It has three views: Kingdoms, Counties, and
  Battle odds, which colours every county by your chance of taking it.

## Crowns, courts and a wider world (v1.2)

- **The line of succession** is shown in Kin: who wears the crown, who is
  first, second and third in line, and why (in historical mode sons inherit
  before daughters, then the eldest). When a monarch dies a card explains who
  inherits, and you choose: kneel, ask for lands of your own, claim the crown
  with the barons behind you, or leave court. Regencies, royal babies, wicked
  uncles and nephews on the throne are all possible. The game warns you before
  anything costs you your claim (holy vows, abdication, fleeing abroad); your
  job never does.
- **Royal life**: sit on the council, win barons over, plot against those
  ahead of you, host tourneys, travel abroad as envoy, hear petitions, and go
  on royal progresses.
- **The Court** (Realm, Court): everyone can see the king's council. Rule a
  kingdom and you choose your own Chancellor, Lord Treasurer, Marshal,
  Spymaster, Chaplain, Physician and Jester: hire from candidates, appoint
  relatives, or dismiss them (and live with the consequences). Lords keep a
  smaller household. Rulers also set taxes, watch the realm's unrest, issue
  decrees, and build great works such as cathedrals, universities, roads and
  harbours.
- **Holdings you run**: 55 things to own, from geese to a silver mine and a
  stone castle. Each grows through three levels, has a plan to choose every
  year (breed or sell, plant a risky cash crop, water the ale...), and can be
  given a manager.
- **Your counties**: set rents, build markets, mills, churches, schools and
  barracks, and keep your people content, or face a rising.
- **A wider world**: new lives begin in nine kingdoms and 89 counties, each
  county known for something (silver mines, abbeys, ports, haunted ruins).
  The map can be zoomed to fit the screen.
- **Twice the life**: 286 occupations on dozens of career roads (pirate to
  admiral, constable to high sheriff, burgess to lord mayor, friar to papal
  legate, Templar to grand master, hedge witch to court astrologer, and many
  more), shown in a new Roads tab; 448 events, many with stories that unfold
  over years; 76 deeds, including hobbies and skills you level up, travel and
  social life; 102 kinds of special work; commissions with deadlines; 86 seals
  of fame; 16 ambitions; and 20 Trials, hard goals for a whole life (Menu).

## Lands, love, fleets and high office (v1.3)

- **Treason has a price.** Attack land sworn to your own king and you are
  declared a traitor at once: you lose any post in his service, your
  knighthood and your barony. Take his royal seat and the crown is yours anyway.
- **Tidier pages.** Every long page (Work, Deeds, Menu, Holdings, Court,
  Lands) folds into sections you open and close like dropdowns, with a count
  on each. The game remembers which ones you left open.
- **Your lands** (Realm, Lands): each county has sliders for taxes, how much
  to reinvest, and how many men to keep under arms, plus a focus (farms,
  trade, walls or the Church) that slowly builds it up. Station a lord, a
  relative or your spouse to run it; their skill and character change the
  results. Rulers see their vassal lords with loyalty and dues, and can honour,
  replace or dispossess them. At the end of each year a report shows what
  every county paid, how its people feel, and what was built.
- **Battles in rounds.** After choosing a plan you order the clash (throw in
  reserves, hold the line, flank with horse, feign a retreat) and then the
  decisive moment (lead the charge, single combat with their champion, press
  on, or retreat to save your men). Feast your soldiers to keep them loyal.
- **Courtship.** Suitors come to court, most of all to royals and nobles, each
  bringing something different: peace with a foreign kingdom, the great lords'
  support, a fortune, fame, or love. Write letters, send gifts, walk together,
  hold a feast in their honour, then ask for their hand. Your spouse's
  character, your children and your friends now change your years.
- **Fleets.** Pirates, smugglers, privateers, ship's captains and admirals
  command real ships (cogs, caravels, galleys, great carracks): raid coastal
  counties, hunt merchantmen, sell plunder, ransom prisoners, keep a crew fed
  and loyal, and live with Notoriety or royal favour.
- **Trade runs.** Merchants read the year's prices for eight goods across six
  markets, pick a stake, and face the road: pirates, storms, tolls, rich buyers
  and cheating factors. Keep a warehouse, take contracts, join the merchants'
  guild, and lend to the king.
- **High office.** 71 top posts ask for more (age, years of experience,
  Wits, Renown and Influence), and promotion up a ladder now asks the same.
  Each year in office brings a duty to perform, chosen from three (126 in all),
  with rewards for doing it well: a stipend, a patron, fame, even land.
- **Every life your own.** 507 events, many written for one station, and more
  of them come up for the life you are living. Roads of fortune (Menu) shows
  every way up, down and sideways from where you stand.

## A living world (v1.4)

- **The world moves on its own.** Every kingdom's monarch ages, marries, has
  heirs and dies; a child may be crowned under a regent, and a death with no
  heir can start a civil war. Kingdoms declare wars that last for years, fought
  county by county on the map, and end in peace treaties. Plague, famine and
  murrain break out and spread from county to county. Famous folk (a champion
  knight, a preacher, a pirate, a merchant prince, a scholar, an outlaw, a
  troubadour) do things everyone talks about, and the year has its weather.
  News reaches your chronicle, and Realm, World shows it all.
- **Your neighbours.** The same people live around you year after year: the
  priest, the reeve, the smith, the alewife, or for the great, a household of
  steward, cook, nurse and fool. They marry, quarrel, have children, die and
  pass on their trades. Tap one to chat, help, ask a favour or quarrel. Your
  friends and family lead lives of their own too: weddings, babies, ruin,
  and marching off to war.
- **A moving picture.** A small illuminated scene of your home sits at the top
  of the page and changes with your station (hovel, town, manor, castle, abbey,
  camp, ship, greenwood, prison) and with the world: seasons, weather, smoke
  from a war, crows in a plague year, a comet.
- **The world comes to you.** The king's levy, war taxes, raiders, refugees,
  the plague next door, crusades, coronations and the famous folk passing
  through. Rulers deal with the real foreign monarchs in Foreign courts:
  embassies, alliances, royal matches, truces and tribute.
- **A life that fits your choices.** In disguise, strangers see a man called
  Jack. At sea, home life gives way to a sailor's life and letters from home.
  Suitors fit your station, and nobody courts a nun.
- **Work in tabs.** My job, Find work and Career roads.

## Careers have steps (v1.5)

A post now needs the one below it on its career road: you serve as a Castle
Guard (or a soldier, or in the town watch) before the Royal Guard, as a Pirate
before you captain a pirate ship, as a Prior before you are made Abbot, and as
a Wealthy Merchant before you lead the guild. Years of training from deeds,
mentors or neighbours still count toward experience, but they no longer stand
in for having done the job. There are ways around it where history had them:
noble birth opens officers' and courtiers' posts, and the king's favour
(Renown 60 and Influence 40) or a patron who thinks well of you can open
others. Every post's page shows the step below it, and Work, Career roads
shows the whole ladder. 151 of 286 posts have a step below; every post can
still be reached from a first job.

## Women's roads, and the prince's eye (v1.12)

Under historical rules a woman can now climb to the top in her own name,
without passing as a man, by the roads women really walked in the Middle
Ages. The bench, the pulpit, the universities and the army stay closed to
women, as they were.

- **The queen's household:** a laundress can become Laundress to the Queen,
  then Chamberer, Damsel of the Queen's Chamber, Mistress of the Queen's
  Chamber, and at last First Lady of the Queen's Household.
- **The royal nursery:** a nursemaid or wet nurse can become Rocker of the
  Royal Cradle, Nurse to the Royal Children, Lady Mistress of the Royal
  Nursery, and Royal Governess.
- **Holy women:** Lay Sister (no dowry asked), Beguine and Grand Mistress of
  the Beguinage, Hospital Sister and Mistress of the Hospital, and above an
  Abbess, the Princess-Abbess of a great royal abbey.
- **Skilled women:** Sworn Midwife and Midwife to the Queen, Silkwoman to the
  Crown, the Queen's Embroideress, Illuminator, Household Governess and Woman
  of Letters.
- **Friends in high places:** tutor children for a fee to build up teaching
  experience on the side, seek a patroness or patron, and ask a patron who
  thinks well of you for a place. A great lady may also notice a hard worker
  and take her up.
- **Old dead ends fixed:** the Royal Governess and the Astronomer used to need
  a post that only men could hold first. Now there are ways in for women.
- **The prince's eye:** a rare chance for a very pretty village girl. A prince
  sees her and brings her to the palace with a place in the queen's
  household. Court him every year (Deeds, or the prince on the Kin page) and
  win over the royal family. If his heart cools, she is sent home to her
  village. If it does not, he may ask the king for her hand, marry her in
  secret, or keep her as his mistress until he weds a princess. A prince who
  becomes king can make her queen. Romance is between adults only and fades
  to black.

### Clearer work (v1.12.4)

- **Back to your old post.** If you are dismissed (say, the king turns you
  out of the royal guard), you can apply again: having held the post counts
  as the step below it, and an old hand is far more likely to be taken back.
- **Where Renown comes from.** Every Renown requirement, and tapping Renown,
  now names the deeds you can do right now that win it (a feast, a tourney,
  calling in a favour, your trade's special work...), plus promotions and
  brave choices.
- **Renown and Standing explained.** Renown is your fame across the realm and
  goes with you everywhere. Standing (on the Work page) is only how your
  masters rate you in your current job: high gets you promoted, very low gets
  you dismissed, and it starts again in each new job.
- **Serfs are bound to the manor.** A serf can farm, herd, labour, serve in
  the lord's house, be levied to fight, or enter religion. Town offices, the
  sea, the crown's service, the universities, the royal household and the
  king's guard now need you to be free first (about 70 posts). Taking holy
  vows frees a serf, as the lord had to give leave.
- **Find work shows only what you could hold.** Under historical rules men
  no longer see women's posts and women no longer see men's. A woman who
  passes as a man sees the soldiering, sea and labouring posts she could
  take. Relaxed rules still show everything.

## One law for the poor, another for the great (v1.11)

- **The punishment fits the crime**, as medieval law had it. Petty theft,
  poaching and brawling: a fine, or the stocks or the whip if you cannot pay
  (a year and a day in the forest gaol for poaching). False weights: the
  pillory, with your goods burned under your nose. Adultery and witchcraft:
  the church court and public penance. Theft, robbery, arson and murder are
  felonies: the rope, branding, or years in the dungeon if the rope is
  commuted. A relapsed heretic, or a wife who kills her husband, is burned;
  a traitor dies a traitor's death.
- **Rank decides how the law treats you.** No court can try a king (the
  realm and the Church take note). A prince or princess is judged by the
  crown, pardoned if loved, otherwise confined in a castle or exiled; for
  treason, perhaps the block. Lords are tried by their peers, pay fines and
  blood money (killing a peasant costs them a few pounds), are beheaded
  rather than hanged, and are attainted and exiled for treason. Clergy, and
  anyone who can read the neck verse once, go to the bishop's court, which
  cannot shed blood. The manor court never puts a lord's child in the stocks.
- **The old ways out of a hanging:** a jury of your neighbours (your friends
  help, your enemies hurt), oath-helpers for the lesser charges, benefit of
  clergy, trial by combat, turning approver against your accomplices, or a
  dash for sanctuary and abjuring the realm.
- **A sentence is served.** Escape is hard, a failed escape means a flogging
  and more years, breaking gaol is itself a hanging felony, and only the
  powerful can buy their release.

## Blood and law (v1.10)

- **Fights by skill.** Health, years in arms, a soldier's or knight's training,
  age and illness decide who wins a fight, and friends at your side help. Every
  fight costs health, even the ones you win, and a hard fight can kill you.
- **Murder has a price.** A hired killer costs what the victim is worth (a
  serf's life is cheap, a lord's is not), so the poor must kill with their own
  hands, in a real fight they can lose. Every killing brings the coroner's
  inquest, a blood feud with the dead one's kin, frightened neighbours and,
  after more than one, the sheriff's hue and cry. Each death makes the next one
  easier to trace.
- **Trials.** People who love you can swear for you as oath-helpers, and your
  enemies (rivals, people around you who hate you, feuds) speak against you.
  Trial by combat is a real fight against the court's champion.
- **Sentences and prison.** Fines for small crimes if you can pay, the debtors'
  gaol or the cart's tail if you cannot, years in the dungeon for theft and
  violence, and the gallows or a long sentence for murder. In prison: a
  cellmate, the gaoler's garnish, gaol fever, the bully of the yard, visitors
  (only those who love you come) and a loose stone in the wall. Coming out, a
  cold spouse may be gone.
- **Know your place.** People around you start from what your station is to
  theirs: a lord is cold to his serfs, the poor are kind to the poor. Feelings
  drift back to that unless you earn better. You cannot just chat with your
  betters, and a serf's gift means little to a lord. Each year your lord,
  your master, your enemies and your friends act on what they feel: doubled
  boon-work or a charter of freedom, promotion or dismissal, informing or a
  timely warning.

## Storylines, the people around you, fear and love (v1.9)

- **Storylines.** Twenty-six tales that run through a life, a chapter every two
  to five years (a random gap), each choice deciding where it goes and how it
  ends: the Pretender, the Royal Favourite, the Hostage Prince, the Court
  Alchemist, the Lord Protector, the Envoy's Secrets, the Peasants' Charter, the
  Rival Knight, the Free Company, the Vow of the Cross, Half a Map, the Captain
  Who Hunts You, the Corsair, the Spice Venture, the Guild War, the Forbidden
  Book, the Girl Who Hears Voices, the Great Mortality, the Secret of the
  Cloister, the Lord's Heir, the Rising, the Outlaw of the Greenwood, the Cunning
  Woman, the Foundling, the Love Letters and the Stranger at the Crossroads. At
  most two run at once, a year with a chapter has no other random event, some
  pass to your heir, and Deeds and your gravestone list how each one ended.
- **Around you** (Kin). The people you live and work beside, each with a
  character and often a secret: the king, the queen, the heir and the scheming
  younger prince at court; your own court's archbishop, envoy and ambitious
  cousin; the captain, mate, bosun and crew aboard ship; the sergeant and your
  comrades in camp; the abbot and the novices in the cloister; the guildmaster,
  the moneylender and the urchin in town; the lord of the manor and his steward;
  the band in the greenwood. Talk, give gifts, ask favours, learn secrets, help
  them sin (carry the queen's letters to her lover, arrange the king's
  meetings, take a cut of the theft), blackmail them, betray them, join the
  prince's plot to the throne, lead a mutiny, or have them murdered. Some come
  to you with their own schemes. Getting caught can mean the Tower, the lash,
  or the gallows.
- **Fear and love** (Realm, Court). A ruler may put anyone in the realm to
  death: kin, councillors, lords, governors. Dread keeps the realm quiet while
  you are strong; love keeps it loyal when you are not. The people you wrong
  are listed by name and wait for their chance: plots, assassins, risings, an
  interdict. Conquered kingdoms remember who they were: show your face, rule
  them gently or wed your heir into their old royal house and they come to
  accept you; neglect them, tax them hard or hold too many at once and they
  rise under their old dynasty and break away.
- **Passions and scandals** (Deeds). Take a mistress or a lover, marry for love
  in secret, seek an annulment, visit the stews, buy a love potion, pay court in
  the courtly fashion, keep a priest's "housekeeper", marry a rich old widow.
  Under historical rules a king's mistress is a fact of court life, while a
  queen's or a wife's lover must stay secret and being found out is ruin.
  Things happen around you too: a royal adultery scandal, a masque that catches
  fire, rough music under your window, the king's eye on your wife, a
  half-brother at the door, a runaway heiress, a mocking song. Everyone
  involved in romance is an adult, and nothing is described.

## Dark deeds, brides and usurpers (v1.8)

- **Dark deeds** (Deeds, Dark deeds) for every walk of life. A monarch can lock
  a rival sibling in the Tower, attaint a lord, debase the coin, harry a
  county, seize loans or poison a foreign heir. Knights can burn the
  countryside, sack an abbey or sell their lance; pirates can give no quarter,
  maroon a crew, torch a port or bury a hoard; traders cheat weights, hoard
  grain and burn a rival's shop; clergy forge relics and skim tithes; anyone
  can poison or blackmail. Penance (Deeds) washes some of it away.
- **Infamy.** Dark deeds raise Infamy. High Infamy brings plots, revenge,
  names like "the Cruel" or "the Wicked", and excommunication costs piety
  every year. It fades slowly, faster for the pious.
- **Royal matches.** An unmarried monarch is brought candidates (always
  adults): a foreign princess or prince for an alliance, a noble heiress or
  heir, a rich merchant's child, or a famous beauty. Any monarch, old or
  young, can seek a match (Deeds, Love and family).
- **Mistresses and favourites.** A ruler can take an acknowledged favourite
  or keep it discreet. Favourites ask for favours, spouses grow jealous, and
  natural children (FitzRoy) are born outside the line of succession unless
  you pay to legitimise them.
- **Usurpers beware.** Seize the throne and the family you deposed hates you,
  the realm is restless, every foreign court thinks less of you, and the old
  crown's allies may declare war. Deposed kin can escape, raise armies from
  exile and come back for the crown for years. Taking the capital means
  beating the royal host that defends it.

## Wars between kingdoms (v1.7)

- **Declared wars.** As a ruler, attacking a kingdom declares war on it. Its
  allies by marriage come to its defence (most of the time), neighbours may
  join if you have grown far stronger, and a court already at war with your
  enemy may fight beside you. Your own ally may march with you. Turning on an
  ally or breaking a truce costs honour everywhere.
- **The royal host.** Every crown at war keeps an army that relieves its
  capital and the towns near it, with its allies' contingents. Beat it in the
  field and it breaks; it recovers a little every year. You cannot demand the
  surrender of a town an army is coming to relieve.
- **A capital that fights on.** Taking the royal seat ends a kingdom only if
  it has three counties or fewer left, the war is nearly won, or its monarch
  is caught (one time in five). Otherwise the court flees to another town and
  the war goes on.
- **They fight back.** Enemies invade your border counties every year of the
  war, and each year of war adds to unrest.
- **Peace.** A war score tracks who is winning. At the enemy's court: offer
  peace, demand terms (a border county and reparations), buy peace, or offer an
  enemy's ally a separate peace. Beaten crowns sue for peace; winning ones send
  demands.
- **Talking to crowns.** Meet a monarch in person: flattery, plain talk, faith,
  a show of arms or a rich gift each please a different kind of crown. Ask a
  court to join your war, or warn one to stay out. A friend of your enemy may
  send an ultimatum, and a hostile neighbour may attack while you are busy.
- **Sellswords.** Only so many captains sign each year (more with Renown), each
  hire costs more than the last, and hiring in wartime costs a quarter more.

## Born to it (v1.6)

- **Rank opens doors.** A prince or princess is handed commands and offices
  (knight, captain, admiral, chamberlain, royal adviser, castellan) without
  years in the ranks, younger than anyone else could hold them, and their name
  counts for 30 Renown towards a post. Nobles need half the usual years and get
  12. Wits, Looks and the rest still count.
- **Prove yourself.** A post your birth handed you starts with low standing.
- **Beneath your rank.** Royals cannot labour, serve, trade in the street or
  enlist as common soldiers, and nobles cannot do menial work. Pages and the
  Church are open to anyone.
- **Live under a false name** (Deeds, Daring) to set your rank aside and take
  humble work. A courtier may recognise you; take back your name to return.
- **The crown's own blood.** A king or queen who loves you may send for you and
  offer you a post.
