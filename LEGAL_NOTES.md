# Staying clear of BitLife and other life-sim games

*This is general information gathered from public sources, not legal advice.
Before you sell the game, have an intellectual-property lawyer review the name,
the store listing and the screenshots. It is a short, inexpensive review compared
with the cost of a dispute.*

## What BitLife's owner actually holds

BitLife is made by **Candywriter, LLC** (owned by Stillfront Group). From public
trademark records:

| Mark | U.S. registration | Covers |
|---|---|---|
| **BITLIFE** (the word) | Reg. No. 5,757,006 (filed 2018, registered May 2019) | Game software |
| BitLife **logo** (a stylized sperm cell) | Reg. No. 5,966,199 (registered January 2020), still live | Downloadable game software |

So **the name "BitLife" and their logo are protected**. Their game's text,
artwork, icons and code are protected by copyright automatically.

## What the courts say about game clones

- **Rules and ideas are free for anyone to use.** In *Tetris Holding v. Xio
  Interactive* (2012), the court said there is no copyright in game mechanics or
  rules. "Live a life year by year, choose actions, face random events" is a
  genre, not property.
- **The look and feel is protected.** The same court found that Xio infringed
  because it copied Tetris's visual expression: the look of the pieces, the
  screen layout and other specific details. In *Spry Fox v. Lolapps* (2012), a
  clone that copied Triple Town's look and feel faced a claim that survived in
  court, and the copier settled.
- **The genre is crowded and legal.** Text life simulators existed before
  BitLife (InstLife was one of the first; Candywriter later bought it), and many
  others sell today.

**In short:** you may make a life simulator. You may not copy BitLife's name,
logo, art, wording, or the specific look and layout of its screens.

## How Mud & Crowns stays clear

| BitLife's expression | Mud & Crowns |
|---|---|
| Name "BitLife", sperm-cell logo | "Mud & Crowns", a wax seal and crown icon |
| Modern life, dark blue and green app look | Medieval life drawn as an illuminated manuscript on parchment |
| Round "+ Age" button in the centre of a 5-button bar | "Turn the page" tab in the corner of the chronicle; 4 bookmark ribbons |

copied from BitLife.

## Rules to keep following

1. **Never use the word "BitLife"** (or "Bit Life", "BitLife-like", "medieval
   BitLife") in your app name, subtitle, keywords, description, screenshots, ads,
   or ad keywords. App Store guideline 4.1 (Copycats) rejects apps whose
   metadata refers to another app, and Google Play's impersonation policy
   forbids names, icons or descriptions that suggest a link to another app.
   This is the most common way small games get removed.
2. **Don't name the game "____Life".** Avoid names close to theirs, such as
   anything with "Bit", or "MedievalLife". "Mud & Crowns" is fine.
3. **Keep your own look.** Don't change the layout back towards theirs (a big
   round age button in the middle of a bottom bar, coloured stat bars at the
   bottom, a dark blue and green theme). Keep the parchment book design.
4. **Write your own words.** Never copy event text, achievement names or menu
   wording from another game, even "as a placeholder".
5. **In marketing, describe your game on its own terms**: "a medieval life
   simulator", "live a whole life from serf to sovereign". Don't compare it to
   another game by name.
6. **Search your own name** in the USPTO trademark search
   (https://tmsearch.uspto.gov) and in both app stores before launch. Once the
   game earns money, consider registering "Mud & Crowns" as your own trademark.
7. **Keep your records.** The git history in this repository shows the game was
   built independently, step by step. Don't delete it.
8. **If you ever get a takedown notice**, don't panic and don't ignore it. Stop
   using whatever it points to, and talk to a lawyer before replying.

## Fonts and art

- EB Garamond and UnifrakturMaguntia are under the SIL Open Font License. You
  may bundle them and sell the app. Keep the license note in `fonts/fonts.css`.
- The portraits, coat of arms, map and app icon are drawn by the game's own code
  and belong to you.
- Small pictures in the menus are system emoji, drawn by the phone itself, so
  nothing needs licensing.
- If you add bought art later, check that its license allows **commercial use in
  a mobile app**, and keep a copy of the license.
